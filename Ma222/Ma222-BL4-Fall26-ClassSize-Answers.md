# Answers: Mean Class Size

Suppose a college is offering five sections of an introductory statistics course. The enrollments for the sections are:

| Section | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Enrollment | 200 | 35 | 35 | 20 | 10 |

**a) What are the observational units? What is the variable?**

The observational units are the **5 sections** of the course. The variable is the **enrollment** (number of students) in each section.

**b) Calculate the mean enrollment per section.**

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{200 + 35 + 35 + 20 + 10}{5} = \frac{300}{5} = 60 \text{ students}
$$

**c) Mean class size from the students' point of view**

Now there are 300 observational units (students). Each student reports the size of their own class:

| Section | Students reporting | Value each reports | Total |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | 200 | 200 | 40,000 |
| 2 | 35 | 35 | 1,225 |
| 3 | 35 | 35 | 1,225 |
| 4 | 20 | 20 | 400 |
| 5 | 10 | 10 | 100 |
| **All** | **300** | | **42,950** |

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{42{,}950}{300} \approx 143.2 \text{ students}
$$

The mean is much bigger from the students' point of view. Most students (200 of 300) are in the large section, so a typical student is in a big class, even though the typical section is much smaller. Which mean you get depends on what the observational units are.

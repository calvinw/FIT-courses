# Answers: Mean Class Size

Suppose a college is offering five sections of an introductory statistics course. The class sizes for the sections are:

| Section | 301 | 302 | 303 | 304 | 305 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Class size | 10 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 6 |

**a) The 5 sections are the observational units.**

The observational units are the **5 sections**. The variable is the **class size** of each section.

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{10 + 4 + 5 + 3 + 6}{5} = \frac{28}{5} = 5.6 \text{ students}
$$

**b) The 28 students are the observational units.**

The observational units are the **28 students**. The variable is the **class size of the student's section**. Each student reports the class size of their own section:

| Section | Students reporting | Value each reports | Total |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| 301 | 10 | 10 | 100 |
| 302 | 4 | 4 | 16 |
| 303 | 5 | 5 | 25 |
| 304 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| 305 | 6 | 6 | 36 |
| **All** | **28** | | **186** |

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{186}{28} \approx 6.64 \text{ students}
$$

The mean is bigger from the students' point of view. More students are in the bigger sections (10 of the 28 are in section 301), so a typical student is in a bigger section than a typical section has. Which mean you get depends on what the observational units are.

# Answers: Two Surveys from 1987

## Shere Hite Survey

a) **Population:** all American women in relationships with men. This is the
group Hite drew conclusions about when she argued that dissatisfaction among
American women was widespread.

b) **Sample:** the approximately 4,500 women who returned completed
questionnaires. **Sample size:** $n = 4{,}500$. The 100,000 questionnaires
Hite distributed are the number sent out, not the sample.

c) **No.** This is a voluntary response sample: the women decided for
themselves whether to answer. Women with strong feelings — especially strong
negative feelings — are far more likely to take the trouble to fill out and
return a long questionnaire, so the respondents are not a cross-section of
American women. The questionnaires were also distributed largely through
women's organizations rather than to a random selection of women. The results
describe the women who chose to answer, not American women generally.

d) $4{,}500 / 100{,}000 = 4.5\%$. About 95.5% of the women who received a
questionnaire never returned one, which leaves enormous room for the
non-respondents to differ from the respondents.

## Washington Post--ABC News Poll

a) **Population:** all adults in the United States.

b) **Sample:** the 1,505 adults interviewed nationwide by telephone.
**Sample size:** $n = 1{,}505$.

c) The difference comes from **how each sample was selected**, not from how
large it was. Hite's 4,500 respondents were self-selected out of 100,000
questionnaires, a 4.5% response rate, and the women who chose to respond were
disproportionately those unhappy enough to want to say so. That bias does not
shrink as the sample gets bigger — a larger voluntary response sample is just a
larger biased sample. The Washington Post--ABC News poll drew a random sample
of adults nationwide, which is why it can report a margin of sampling error of
about $\pm 3$ percentage points; Hite's survey cannot honestly report one at all.

Two smaller differences are also worth noting. The surveys studied different
populations: Hite surveyed women only, while the poll interviewed adults of both
sexes. They also used different methods — an anonymous written questionnaire
versus a telephone interview with a live person — and people are generally less
willing to admit relationship problems to an interviewer than on paper, so the
poll's very positive results may themselves be somewhat overstated. Even so,
selection bias in Hite's sample is the main explanation for the gap.

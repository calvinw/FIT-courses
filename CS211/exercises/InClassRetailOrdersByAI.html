Retail Orders: Views with AI

This time Gemini writes the SQL, and you save the queries you like as views in your own copy of the retail orders database. A view is a saved query with a name. Once it is saved, anyone can run it with SELECT * FROM view_name; without knowing the SQL behind it.

Copy each block (use the copy button in its top right corner) and paste it into Gemini. Run the blocks in the same chat: the table schemas tell Gemini what your tables look like, so they have to go first.

Step 1: Fork the Database

  1. Sign in to DoltHub.
  2. Open the retail orders database: https://www.dolthub.com/repositories/calvinw/retail-orders
  3. Click Fork in the top right corner. This makes your own copy of the database under your DoltHub account.
  4. Open your fork. Its address looks like https://www.dolthub.com/repositories/YOUR-USERNAME/retail-orders. Do all of your work in your fork, not in the original.

Step 2: Give Gemini the Table Schemas

Paste this into Gemini first. It shows the three tables, their columns, and how they connect.

CREATE TABLE customers (
  customer_id int NOT NULL,
  first_name varchar(50) NOT NULL,
  last_name varchar(50) NOT NULL,
  email varchar(100) NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (customer_id)
);

CREATE TABLE products (
  product_id int NOT NULL,
  name varchar(100) NOT NULL,
  price decimal(10,2) NOT NULL,
  description varchar(100) NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (product_id)
);

-- order_date holds a date and a time, like 2025-03-10 14:30:00.
-- Each order is for one product.
CREATE TABLE orders (
  order_id int NOT NULL,
  customer_id int NOT NULL,
  product_id int NOT NULL,
  order_date timestamp,
  PRIMARY KEY (order_id),
  FOREIGN KEY (customer_id) REFERENCES customers (customer_id),
  FOREIGN KEY (product_id) REFERENCES products (product_id)
);

Step 3: Save 4 Views

Pick 4 of the questions below. For each one, have Gemini write the query, run it in your fork, and check that the result really answers the question. Then save it as a view, named for what it shows, like customer_spending.

Worked Example

Show the name and price of every product Jane Smith ordered, sorted from lowest price to highest.

The answer should be the Cotton T-Shirt, the Silk Blouse, and the Cashmere Sweater, in that order. Saved as a view, it could be named jane_smith_products.

Choose 4 Questions

Pick 4 of the questions below and save each one as a view in your fork. At least 2 of your 4 must come from the Harder Questions.

Questions

1. Mid-March Shoppers

Show the first_name and last_name of every customer who placed an order between 2025-03-15 and 2025-03-21. List each customer only once.

2. Big-Ticket Orders

For every order of a product that costs more than $80, show the customer's first_name, the product's name, and the order_date.

3. Revenue by Product

For each product that was ordered at least once, show its name and total_revenue, its price times the number of times it was ordered, sorted from highest total_revenue to lowest.

4. Repeat Customers

Show the first_name and num_orders of every customer who placed more than one order.

5. No T-Shirt

Show the first_name and last_name of every customer who has never ordered the Cotton T-Shirt.

Harder Questions

6. Customer Spending Summary

For every customer, including customers who have never ordered, show the first_name, last_name, num_orders, and total_spent. Show 0 for customers with no orders. Sort from highest total_spent to lowest.

7. Products Nobody Bought

Show the name and price of every product that has never been ordered, sorted from highest price to lowest.

8. Monthly Sales

For each month, show the month as YYYY-MM, num_orders, the number of orders that month, and revenue, the total price of those orders, sorted by month.

9. Above-Average Spenders

Show the first_name, last_name, and total_spent of every customer whose total spending is more than the average total spending of the customers who placed orders.

10. Product Popularity Ranking

For every product, including products that were never ordered, show the name, times_ordered, and popularity_rank, where the most ordered product is rank 1 and products tied on times_ordered get the same rank.

11. Bought It Again

Show the first_name, last_name, product name, and times_ordered for every customer who ordered the same product more than once.

12. Each Customer’s First Order

For each customer who has placed an order, show the first_name, last_name, the name of the product in their first order, and the order_date of that first order, sorted by order_date.

What to Hand In

The link to your fork, with your 4 views committed to main.
